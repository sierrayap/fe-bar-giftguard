The problem

Gift cards are cash.

Stolen cards, account takeover, people buying a stack of max-denomination cards and dumping them. You don't catch it at the till — you catch it weeks later as a chargeback and a queue your analysts work by hand.

  • Not a generic "improve operations" story — a specific one: stop bad gift-card orders at purchase without blocking the person just buying a birthday card.
  • The money shows up as chargebacks and merchant penalties, long after the order already happened.
  • Customer here is a digital gift-card platform — think Prezzee. Bunnings cards, Woolies cards, redeemed from an account.

  • I'm watching precision-recall, not accuracy. Fraud is ~1.5% of orders — accuracy would look brilliant and tell you nothing.
  • Chargebacks show up weeks after the order. Training on them would look like a genius at training time and be useless at checkout.
  • Two bands on purpose: block is high confidence, review is the grey area. We're not declining a real customer on a 0.4 score.

Business

Gift cards are cash, so fraud is expensive. We score every order, block the obvious fraud, send the grey area to a review queue, and give the analyst a one-line reason for each case. On ~100,000 orders we blocked $348,000, and $226,000 of that was confirmed fraud. 80% of fraud orders got caught somewhere. The thing to tune is how many good customers end up in review — that's a dial, not a rebuild.

Technical

Raw JSON lands in a UC volume. Lakeflow uses Auto Loader into bronze streaming tables, then silver/gold materialized views with expectations. Gold features feed an XGBoost classifier tracked in MLflow, registered in UC. A job batch-scores gold, writes decisions and ai_query summaries back. PII is masked in UC. Flagged scores load into Lakebase for point lookups. Genie sits on gold. The app is a Databricks App reading the warehouse and Lakebase. Everything is an asset bundle — it redeploys the same way every time.

Backup — if they ask

Objection handling

Cost?
All serverless, pay for what runs. Pipeline and scoring are batch, minutes per run. Lakebase scales down when quiet. Size it off your volumes in the pilot.
How many good customers do you annoy?
~5% of legit orders went to review or block, mostly review, so a person looks first. Move the threshold and we trade catch rate for fewer flags — we can show exactly what each setting costs.
How sure are you about $2.4M?
Not quoting it as a result — it's a model on a $4M fraud book. What I stand behind today: $226,000 of $237,000 in fraud stopped at block, this run. The pilot turns the model into your number.
How long until it's live?
Pattern already exists. Work is plugging in your order feed and agreeing the threshold. A few weeks for a pilot on history, then a shadow period before it touches a real customer.
Is this real-time?
Not yet. Today scoring is batch, loaded into Lakebase for fast lookup. Real-time at checkout means a Model Serving endpoint behind the already-registered UC model — a deployment step, not a rebuild.
Why Lakebase, not just Delta?
Delta is for scans and aggregates. Checkout needs one row by order ID in milliseconds — Lakebase gives you Postgres for that. In your account, a synced table keeps it current automatically.
Data quality?
Expectations in the pipeline drop bad records before scoring. Chargebacks are deliberately left out of model features — they show up weeks late and would leak the label.
Security / PII?
Column masks in UC on email, IP, and card last-four. Only fraud-ops sees real values. Genie and the app share the same permissions — no side door.
Drift?
Labels and scores land back in gold, so retraining is a scheduled job. Watch precision-recall weekly; only promote a new UC model version if it beats the current one.
Why not one big streaming pipeline?
Bronze is streaming with Auto Loader. Silver/gold are materialized views because windowed features are simpler and correct as batch — streaming would add state and cost with no change in result.
